> **Precision statement.** Human Energy Enablement is HEE’s proposed framework for investigating organizational conditions that restrict the application of existing capability. It helps readers distinguish missing capability, insufficient available capacity, disconnected capability, and blocked application; establish what evidence is needed; select a proportionate response; and review its consequences. It does not establish a cause from a performance shortfall, guarantee better decisions, or demonstrate that improved application protects future capacity.

> **Evidence status.** HEEn is a developing conceptual framework, not a validated diagnostic instrument. Its six pillars, diagnostic distinctions, and decision aids require testing. Established research informs the framework; it does not validate HEEn’s particular categories or demonstrate incremental usefulness. The author originated HEE and HEEn and has a commercial interest disclosed below. AKG cases are author-reported practice accounts, not independent tests of this framework. The [HEE Core README](https://github.com/titumozammel/human-energy-economics) governs shared terminology where the documents differ.

**Evidence labels:** **[E]** existing research; **[A]** adaptation of existing ideas; **[P]** proposed and untested; **[H]** practice heuristic; **[Illustrative]** invented example. A label describes the status of a claim, not its certainty in every setting. **Unknown** means the available evidence does not support a defensible determination. **Not recorded** means this edition contains no record; it does not mean the event never occurred or that no record exists elsewhere.

## The question HEEn answers

A capable team misses a target. Is the answer more training, more usable time, a better handoff, access to a system, or a different approval arrangement?

> **What organizational condition is preventing available capability from becoming effective application?**

This question opens an investigation; it does not assume that a restriction is the cause. Capability may genuinely be missing, the workload may exceed capacity, the target may be inappropriate, or several conditions may coexist.

**Develop what is missing. Enable what already exists. Protect what the next cycle needs.** These are prompts for judgment, not instructions to apply every intervention.

HEEn’s proposed application pathway is **Capability → Enablement → Application → Execution**. Connection, Human Energy, available capacity, and Decision Process Quality also matter. The pathway expresses functional relationships, not a compulsory sequence or a proven causal chain. For the full conversion logic that places all five conversion conditions, see the [HEE Core README](https://github.com/titumozammel/human-energy-economics#central-conversion-pathway).

## Definitions and distinctions

These definitions follow the closed HEE Core reference available for this revision.

| Term | Working meaning | Practical distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capability | Potential to perform: what a person, team, system, or organization is able to do | A skill can exist without being applied |
| Capacity | The part of capability that can be applied in a stated period given available time, attention, and energy | Concerns how much is available; it is not application itself |
| Usable Capacity | Capacity that can actually be brought to bear on relevant work after demand and drain, subject to connection, decision-process, and enablement conditions | Nominal staffing or idle time alone does not establish usable capacity |
| Human Energy | HEE’s proposed cognitive, emotional, and physical resources for applying capability and sustaining execution | Not a direct physiological measure or synonym for motivation; distinctness remains open to testing |
| Connection | Whether distributed capabilities can reach, combine with, and support one another | A failed expertise link can coexist with blocked authority |
| Organizational Enablement | Conditions permitting application at the point of need: access, authority, information, resources, decision rights, process, and support | Concerns whether capability can be used, not merely how much time exists |
| Application | Putting capability to work | Application need not produce valuable results |
| Execution | Carrying out intended work toward a result | Activity alone does not establish success or sustainability |
| Enablement Gap [P] | Existing required capability is materially restricted from legitimate expected application by organizational conditions | Record insufficient capacity separately; do not infer the gap from low output alone |
| Sustainable Execution Capacity (SEC) [P] | Continuing ability and readiness to execute effectively over time | Requires follow-up evidence; a faster task does not establish SEC |

**A resource can raise two questions.** Too few available work hours is a capacity issue. Existing hours that cannot be used because access is denied can indicate enablement. Missing tools, information, or staffing may create interacting availability and access issues. Record the mechanism and evidence rather than forcing every case into one pillar.

## The Decision Point

*Quoted from the Core README’s The Decision Point (the canonical text, without its illustrative example). If the two differ, the Core README governs.*

Within HEE, consequential decisions along the pathway from capability to sustainable results should be explicit and reviewable. Evaluate them using four distinct definitions:

1. **Decision Enablement:** whether the necessary decision rights, information, authority, and access exist.
2. **Decision Process Quality:** how the decision was reached.
3. **Decision Quality:** whether the choice was appropriate given what could reasonably be known.
4. **Decision Outcome Quality:** what actually happened.

**Model boundary.** Decision Process Quality remains the core model’s conversion condition. These four definitions are complementary assessment lenses, not four additional conversion conditions or compulsory sequential stages.

Assess these separately. A good outcome does not prove that the process or choice was sound; a poor outcome does not automatically establish that either was unsound. Context shapes every decision: its objective, legitimate demand, available evidence, constraints, alternatives, uncertainty, timing, and consequences.

**The Two Checks.** Apply the Two Checks proportionately to decisions affecting capacity:

- **Justified?** Was the expenditure of capacity warranted?
- **Covered?** Can credible expected usable capacity meet legitimate next-cycle demand?

A justified expenditure can still leave future capacity Not covered. Where evidence is insufficient, record Unknown and specify the next evidence request or precautionary action.

**Investigation routes.** Choose the route that fits the question:

- **Diagnose** (the Diagnostic Approach): investigate symptoms, test possible causes, and select a useful intervention.
- **Establish:** gather the evidence needed to make an uncertain question decidable.
- **Verify** (the Audit Approach): compare practice with a stated standard using traceable evidence.

These routes can inform one another; they are not compulsory sequential stages.

**The compressed conversion pathway and evidence boundaries.** Capability → Application → Execution → Value → Sustainable Growth. This compressed pathway does not replace the core model’s Human Energy, Connection, Decision Process Quality, Organizational Enablement, capacity, or renewal conditions. The Decision Point operates throughout this intended pathway. It makes choices and their consequences inspectable; it does not guarantee progression or sustainable results.

Customer Experience outcomes provide important evidence of customer-facing value. Sustainable Growth requires examining CX alongside employee experience, operating results, future-capacity coverage, and wider value consequences over time. Good CX alone does not establish that future delivery can be sustained.


## What HEEn contributes

HEEn proposes a practical organization of established enablement concerns into six areas, a route for separating candidate limiting conditions, and a decision-and-review record linking intervention to application and future coverage. Its intended contribution is better question formulation and more inspectable intervention choices, not discovery of the idea that working conditions affect performance.

Whether this organization improves decision speed, decision quality, diagnostic reliability, or outcomes beyond credible alternatives remains an empirical question. A new vocabulary or a successful example is not sufficient evidence of added value.

## One case from question to review

**All teams, quantities, thresholds, periods, and results below are invented [Illustrative]. This is neither an AKG case nor evidence for HEEn.**

Two sales teams miss quote-turnaround targets. Asking only “Who needs training?” makes training seem inevitable. A better question is: **where does a legitimate quote wait, what capability does it require, and what evidence separates missing skill from blocked application?**

| Step | Team A: suspected blocked approval | Team B: suspected missing skill |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Expected application | Accurate routine quotes within the agreed service window | Same requirement |
| Evidence | Staff correctly complete a representative pricing exercise; sampled quotes wait for weekly director sign-off | Staff make pricing errors even with current information, access, time, and supervision |
| Alternatives | Workload, missing data, exceptional discount risk, and demand complexity still need checking | Access, workload, and unclear rules still need checking |
| Candidate explanation | Decision Enablement restriction | Capability Gap |
| Response to test | Limited delegation for eligible routine quotes, preserving exception review | Targeted instruction and coached practice |
| What would change the view? | Quotes still stall after authority is usable, or errors rise | Staff perform correctly when access or information changes without instruction |

**Team A’s decision.** Retaining weekly approval preserves oversight but maintains delay. Adding training does not directly address the observed queue. A bounded delegation trial can test the authority explanation while preserving controls. The owner approves a two-week trial limited to a named quote class, keeps high-risk exceptions with the director, and names a backup approver.

**Measures set before starting.** Compare ten prior working days with the trial: quote mix and volume, median and upper-tail turnaround, approval waiting, pricing errors, rework, employee after-hours work, customer complaints, and director review effort. A contemporaneous team can help if its work is genuinely comparable; this illustration is not randomized and cannot exclude all confounding.

**Illustrative stop and review rules.** Pause affected delegation if a material control breach occurs. At two weeks, review whether waiting and turnaround improve without an unacceptable increase in errors, effort, or future shortfall. If authority was never operational, the intervention was not implemented and the trial does not test the proposed mechanism. If authority was usable but work still waits, investigate alternatives; do not automatically conclude that staff lack capability.

**Illustrative result.** Median turnaround falls from three working days to one; pricing errors remain unchanged, but overtime rises. This supports a narrower account of reduced waiting, not a claim of sustainable improvement. A simultaneous fall in quote complexity would weaken attribution further. The owner must address overtime and next-cycle coverage before expanding the trial.

**The four decision reviews.** Delegation and backup establish Decision Enablement. The options, evidence, and safeguards describe Decision Process Quality. The trial choice is judged against what was known before it began. Turnaround, errors, and overtime describe Decision Outcome Quality. These assessments may differ.

## Investigate: choose the route

Investigation has three branches. Choose the branch that matches the question; they are not three compulsory stages.

| Branch | Starting question | Method | Required output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Diagnose** | Why is this happening? | Trace symptoms, test candidate causes and alternatives | A supported candidate explanation, uncertainty, and useful response |
| **Establish** | What evidence would let us decide? | Specify the decision, missing evidence, collection plan, and sufficiency rule | A decidable question or an explicit Unknown with a bounded action |
| **Verify** | Does practice meet the stated standard? | Compare sampled practice with a valid, scoped criterion | A supported conformity finding, exception, or inability to conclude |

A delay can justify diagnosis. Missing authority records may first require Establish. Checking compliance with an agreed approval policy calls for Verify. An audit exception can trigger diagnosis, but a failed control does not identify its cause by itself. New evidence may change the route.

## Diagnose: from symptom to supported explanation

**Diagnostic approach [H]: symptom → candidate explanations → discriminating evidence → supported diagnosis → response → review.**

1. **Bound the problem.** Specify the legitimate task, expected application, scope, period, actual symptom, and who is affected. Test whether the target itself is reasonable.
2. **Assess capability and capacity separately.** Use a representative work sample or prior comparable successful work, with suitable access and time. Training attendance, qualifications, confidence, and agreement in interviews are supporting information, not proof of task capability.
3. **Trace actual work.** Follow representative items, including ordinary, failed, and exceptional cases. Record waiting, rework, decisions, access, workload, support, and handoffs with sources and dates.
4. **Test alternatives.** Consider missing skill, unavailable capacity, connection failure, drain, external dependencies, inappropriate demand, and measurement error. Interview agreement identifies a candidate barrier, not an established cause.
5. **Locate the suspected restriction.** Use the pillars below, recording overlap. Keep observations separate from interpretation and retain contradictory evidence.
6. **Test necessity and choose a proportionate response.** Preserve safeguards, check authority, and prefer a small reversible test where suitable.
7. **Review implementation and effects.** Confirm that the condition actually changed before interpreting outcomes. Review quality, costs, harms, workload transfer, and future coverage.

| Evidence pattern | Candidate explanation | Useful next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cannot perform a representative task even with appropriate conditions | Capability Gap | Develop, then reassess application |
| Skill exists but legitimate demand exceeds credible available time or energy | Capacity shortfall or Human Energy issue requiring investigation | Review allocation, demand, protection, recovery, or additional resources |
| Required capabilities cannot reach or combine with one another | Capability Connection Gap | Connect and test the specific handoff or expertise link |
| Capability exists but a named access, authority, information, or support condition blocks use | Enablement Gap | Enable the specific application, subject to necessity checks |
| Activity consumes capacity without sufficient contribution to a verified purpose | Candidate Drain Condition | Investigate demand and use OSF/5R where appropriate |
| A restriction may be unnecessary or disproportionate | Candidate Artificial Constraint Condition | Test purpose and safeguards before changing it |
| Evidence points to several mechanisms, or does not discriminate | Mixed or Unknown | Record each possibility and Establish the next useful evidence |

An Execution Deficit is a trigger for inquiry, not a causal explanation. These are provisional distinctions linked to the core vocabulary, not a validated taxonomy. A response can combine interventions; diagnosing one condition does not exclude others.

## Six enablement pillars

The six pillars are proposed areas for organizing inquiry **[P]**. Indicators and actions below are heuristics **[H]**, not validated scales or proof of causation. No total score, ranking, or pillar maximum is prescribed.

| Pillar | Governing question | Evidence to inspect | Alternative or overlap | Possible bounded action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Resource** | Can the work obtain what it needs at the point of use? | Tool access, licences, material availability, data freshness, actual resource queues | Genuine capacity shortage; missing technical skill | Provide named access or resource, then test its use |
| **Structural** | Do roles, responsibilities, and interfaces permit action? | Responsibility records, handoff traces, duplicate approvals, owner disputes | Connection failure; necessary separation of duties | Clarify an owner or redesign one interface |
| **Cultural** | Do operating norms permit constructive action and challenge? | Confidential accounts triangulated with actual escalation and response patterns | Incentives, power, uncertainty, or an unsuitable target | Change a specific response or escalation practice and check effects |
| **Decision** | Can the needed decision be made with authority and information in time? | Delegations, approval timestamps, queue length, backup arrangements | Weak decision process; incomplete evidence; exceptional risk | Bounded delegation or a usable escalation path |
| **Support** | Is relevant help available when a barrier arises? | Support availability, response records, unresolved dependencies | Skill absence; unsuitable tools; unclear responsibility | Provide accountable support for a defined task |
| **Environmental** | Does the physical, digital, and workflow setting support application? | Usability observations, interruption patterns, accessibility barriers, workflow traces | Excess demand; depleted capacity; specialist safety concerns | Fix one evidenced barrier while preserving safeguards |

Do not classify a culture problem from one person’s silence, or an energy problem from ticket counts. Collect only proportionate information, protect confidentiality, and use relevant specialists for health, safety, legal, or technical questions. HEEn is not an instrument for surveillance or individual performance ranking.

## Establish: from Unknown to a decision

The aim is sufficient evidence for a specified decision, not unlimited collection. Define sufficiency before seeing a preferred result.

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Decision and deadline | What choice must be made, by whom, and when does it lose value? |
| Missing distinction | What uncertainty could change that choice? |
| Discriminating evidence | What observation would favor one explanation over another? |
| Collection | Source, period, sample rationale, collector, access, confidentiality, and cost |
| Sufficiency rule | What evidence supports action, weakens it, or remains inconclusive? |
| Remaining uncertainty | What cannot be settled in time, and what precaution or contingency addresses it? |

If evidence remains weak, keep Unknown visible. Unknown does not mean zero risk, a confirmed gap, or that action must stop. The owner may choose a reversible trial, temporary safeguard, deferral, or escalation, stating the rationale and authority. Do not approve a major enablement investment merely by naming a pillar; equally, do not withhold a necessary protective action just because diagnosis is incomplete.

## Test the constraint before changing it

A slow approval may be necessary; a protective purpose does not establish that its present design is proportionate.

1. Name the restriction and the work it affects.
2. Establish its legitimate purpose, owner, authority, and applicable requirements.
3. Examine evidence that it serves that purpose, including adverse consequences of removing it.
4. Test proportionality: can a less burdensome design preserve the purpose?
5. Record **retain**, **redesign**, **test an authorized alternative**, **escalate**, or **Unknown**, with reasons.
6. If changed, specify replacement safeguards, triggers, monitoring, and reversal authority.

**Prevent** a known avoidable barrier where warranted; **limit** exposure or workload while uncertainty remains; **stop or pause** an intervention that crosses a pre-agreed harm or control threshold. These are situational actions, not a requirement to remove every restriction.

## Verify: practice against a standard

**Audit approach [H]: standard → scoped practice → evidence → finding → corrective action → verification.** This is an internal review guide, not a certification standard or an assurance opinion.

1. Establish the requirement, its authority, version, scope, and applicability. A HEEn pillar is not automatically a standard.
2. Define the workflow, period, population, sample rationale, and reviewer. Use an independent reviewer where consequences or conflicts warrant it; record self-review limits.
3. Collect traceable evidence and relevant exceptions. Inquiries alone may not establish that a control operated.
4. Compare practice with the criterion. Use **conforms**, **does not conform**, **insufficient evidence**, or **not applicable**, with reasons.
5. Separate the observed exception from any proposed cause. Route causal questions to Diagnose.
6. Agree correction, accountability, safeguards, due date, and evidence required for closure.
7. Recheck implementation and effectiveness. An action marked complete is not automatically an effective correction.

### Audit example and reusable record

**Invented policy and records [Illustrative].** Assume an authorized policy requires eligible routine quotes to have a named approver and backup, and an approval decision within one working day. This criterion is hypothetical, not a universal HEEn benchmark.

| Field | Illustrative entry |
| --- | --- |
| Criterion | Current approved policy, named version and effective date; specified quote class |
| Scope and sample | Ten eligible quotes from the review week; purposive sample, not a population estimate |
| Evidence | Approved delegation list, queue timestamps, exception records, and backup availability |
| Finding | Three sampled decisions exceeded the required window; one had no assigned backup |
| Conclusion | Sampled exceptions to the criterion; no claim that all quotes fail |
| Proposed cause | Unconfirmed: backup coverage may be ineffective; investigate separately |
| Correction | Assign usable backup coverage and resolve current overdue items |
| Accountability | Named policy owner, reviewer, due date, and escalation authority |
| Follow-up | Recheck assignments and a new period of eligible quotes; retain open status if evidence is inadequate |

A process can conform to a poorly designed standard and still perform badly. Conversely, a good customer outcome does not establish control conformity. Audit compliance and diagnostic usefulness are separate questions.

## Justified expenditure and future coverage

Do not treat all capacity consumption as loss. Learning, maintenance, recovery, incident response, and deliberate investment can be justified. Equally, justified expenditure can leave an actual future shortfall.

**Check 1 — Justified?** Was the expenditure of capacity warranted given its purpose, alternatives, proportionality, constraints, and what could reasonably be known? Test the necessity of a restriction separately; the two judgments must not be conflated.

**Check 2 — Covered?** Over a stated next-cycle horizon, does credible expected usable capacity cover legitimate demand under plausible scenarios, with the required capability, access, and credible recovery or renewal assumptions?

| Evidence state | Working interpretation | Decision implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Covered** | Legitimate demand is covered across the plausible scenarios reviewed | Proceed with monitoring and explicit assumptions |
| **Fragile** | Base demand is covered but a plausible scenario creates a material gap | Require a feasible contingency, trigger, owner, and review |
| **Not covered** | Credible expected capacity falls materially short of legitimate demand | Reschedule, reduce or redirect demand, add feasible resources, recover or protect capacity, or renegotiate commitments |
| **Unknown** | Evidence cannot support a defensible classification | Establish missing evidence and make an explicit precautionary decision |

Use the core’s full classification protocol for consequential decisions. Do not assign numeric thresholds universally from this table.

**Ordinary justified expenditure [Illustrative].** A team spends 12 hours on necessary maintenance. Credible next-cycle usable capacity is 100 hours against 90 hours of legitimate demand across the scenarios reviewed. Subject to the stated assumptions, the expenditure is justified and coverage is intact. Consumption alone is not evidence of harmful depletion.

**Justified expenditure with a shortfall [Illustrative].** Emergency work is warranted, but next-cycle usable capacity is only 80 hours against 100 hours of legitimate demand; the 20-hour gap is material. Justification does not remove the shortfall. The owner must change commitments or feasible capacity, not label the emergency expenditure unjustified merely because coverage is poor.

Keep units, skill requirements, time horizons, and demand assumptions consistent. Do not double-count recovery both as work time available and as time reserved away from work. Faster approval can expose downstream bottlenecks; reduced waiting does not necessarily release equivalent person-hours.

**Protect any release.** Record whether usable capacity was actually released, where it goes, who owns its destination, which new demands require reassignment approval, and when use is reviewed. Useful redeployment is not automatically failed protection. Record justified reassignment separately from silent reabsorption into low-value work. [Core protection protocol](https://github.com/titumozammel/human-energy-economics#capacity-protection-and-anti-reabsorption-protocol-h).

## Owner decision record

Use a short record for a small reversible decision and a fuller one for consequential or difficult-to-reverse choices **[H]**.

| Field | Decision-ready answer |
| --- | --- |
| Objective and scope | What valuable application is needed, for whom, in what period? |
| Evidence and uncertainty | What do we know, from which sources; what contradicts the preferred explanation? |
| Capability and capacity | Does the skill exist, and how much usable capacity is available? |
| Candidate condition | What is blocked; what alternatives or mixed conditions remain plausible? |
| Options | Retain current practice, enable, develop, connect, protect/recover, change demand, or gather evidence |
| Choice and reasons | Why is this option proportionate given benefits, costs, opportunity costs, and uncertainty? |
| Safeguards | Legitimate controls, adverse-effect measures, stop/pause triggers, and reversal authority |
| Justification and coverage | Was expenditure warranted; is the next cycle Covered, Fragile, Not covered, or Unknown? |
| Responsibility | Decision owner, implementation owner, reviewer, required authority, and due dates |
| Review | Implementation check; application, quality, cost, workload transfer, and future-coverage measures |
| Capacity destination | If release occurs, intended use, owner, reassignment rule, and follow-up |

Do not fill missing fields with invented estimates presented as facts. Label estimates, assumptions, and illustrative figures distinctly.

### Decision speed and review

Use a **minimum useful record** even for a quick choice: question, evidence and key uncertainty, action, owner, safeguard or reversal trigger, and review point. Collect these in the same conversation where feasible. Do not remove traceability simply to move faster.

Escalate depth for substantial costs, safety or regulatory implications, disputed authority, difficult reversibility, or material next-cycle consequences. For urgent uncertainty, document a temporary protective decision and the next evidence checkpoint. These are judgment rules, not validated time-saving claims. [Core Decision Speed Card](https://github.com/titumozammel/human-energy-economics#decision-speed-card-h).

At review, ask in order: **Was the change implemented? Did the proposed barrier change? Did application and valued results change? What happened to costs, harms, and future coverage?** A null result can reflect a false diagnosis, incomplete implementation, poor measurement, insufficient time, or another bottleneck. It does not automatically establish a Capability Gap. Stop or revise as the pre-agreed rules and evidence warrant.

## Employee Experience, Customer Experience, and Sustainable Growth

HEEn proposes this pathway **[P]**: a specific change to enablement conditions may alter employee friction and ability to act; changed application may alter service reliability, quality, or responsiveness; customer consequences and the capacity needed to keep delivering must then be examined over time. Each link needs evidence. Satisfaction scores do not establish the mechanism.

| Link to examine | Proposed example | Evidence and rival explanations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enablement → EX | Usable authority reduces avoidable waiting and frustration | Approval traces, confidential experience accounts; check workload, leadership, or pay changes |
| Enablement → application | Staff can complete eligible work without avoidable escalation | Completed work, error rates, queue movement; check demand mix and capability changes |
| Application → CX | Accurate quotes arrive sooner and customers wait less | Turnaround, customer feedback, complaints, quality; check pricing and customer-mix changes |
| Current delivery → future sustainability | Results can be repeated without hidden overtime, deferred maintenance, or depleted reserves | Repeated coverage assessments, recovery, rework, costs, downstream burdens, and relevant wider consequences |

**Good CX with worsening EX** can indicate effort or hidden overtime sustaining delivery; it can also have other causes. **Improved EX with weak CX** may indicate an external or downstream constraint, a time lag, or a change that helps staff without improving customer value. Neither pattern establishes a cause. Triangulate EX and CX with operating data and evidence from affected teams.

In HEE, Sustainable Growth concerns continuing value creation without undermining the capacity needed for future value, considering consequences across financial, manufactured, intellectual, human, social and relationship, and natural capital. A workflow-level enablement change cannot establish organization-wide Sustainable Growth by itself. Assess scope, duration, external conditions, and transferred costs before making a broader claim.

## Boundaries and architecture

HEE is the provisional capacity lens informed by economic questions. **OEOS** is the proposed operating architecture; **HEMS** is its Human Energy and capability-management component. HEEn supplies the application-specific enablement role. These are proposed constructs, not validated systems. The five conversion conditions remain **Human Energy, Capability, Connection, Decision Process Quality, and Organizational Enablement**; SEC is an intended outcome, not a sixth conversion condition.

| Related element | Its role | HEEn boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HEA / HERF | Examine operating effects and restore usable Human Energy/capacity | HEEn does not directly measure energy or replace recovery |
| HEDP | Develop genuinely missing capability | Enablement cannot supply a skill that is absent |
| Capability Interconnection | Connect distributed capability | Connection and access/authority may coexist; record both where supported |
| OSF and 5R | Examine unnecessary recurring demand and choose Remove, Reduce, Replace, Re-engineer, Retain | HEEn enables legitimate work that remains; it does not decide all demand disposition |
| 3C | Direct attention to Source, Dependency, Constraint | HEEn investigates and changes a particular enabling condition |
| HEL | Examine recurring systemic unnecessary restrictions on flow | HEEn begins with a particular task, role, or application; systemic patterns may need the broader lens |
| EES | Manage execution through Find, Fix, Control, Adapt, Grow | Enabling application is not the same as managing all execution |

Some elements are documented within the core and have no separate repository. Markets, strategy, regulation, technical failures, industrial relations, and health questions may require other explanations or specialists. Do not force them into HEEn.

**Prioritization [H].** The shared expression *Priority = Urgency × Interdependence × Value Impact* is a heuristic, not a validated equation or a meaningful numeric score without defensible scales. Use the factors alongside safety, authority, reversibility, uncertainty, and cost. Interdependence here is not identical to 3C’s Dependency context.

**Growth [P].** Growth may add interfaces and enablement demands, but it can also bring resources, simplification, and scale benefits. Test the conditions rather than assuming complexity must worsen. Service and experience commitments can orient inquiry, but SLA → XLA → HEE → HEEn is not a validated causal sequence.

**Descriptive progression [P].** Constrained, Aware, Responsive, Enabled, Integrated, Adaptive, Sustainable are optional descriptive prompts retained from earlier HEEn editions. They are not an established ordered maturity scale, a certification, or grounds to score organizations or people. Improvement can be uneven and reversible.

## Theory and contribution

Blumberg and Pringle’s opportunity-to-perform account is a close antecedent: their 1982 paper examines opportunity and its interaction with other performance conditions. HEEn adapts this general insight **[E/A]**; it does not claim to have discovered it. The core also discusses situational constraints and associated measurement traditions.

The proposed contribution **[P]** is an application-oriented organization of enablement concerns within HEE: six areas, differential questions, explicit evidence states, proportionate intervention, and review of capacity consequences. Whether this contributes anything beyond established diagnostics must be tested against credible alternatives with comparable evidence and effort.

“Enablement Gap” may substantially overlap established opportunity or constraint constructs. HEEn does not presently demonstrate that it is a distinct latent construct. The six pillars can be a useful indexing scheme even if they are not empirically separable factors; failure of factor separation would weaken a six-dimension measurement claim, not automatically disprove the usefulness of every checklist item.

Organizational application is a proposed level of use, not proof of a novel theoretical extension. Specify how individual observations relate to workflow or organizational conditions, what shared evidence supports aggregation, and whether multilevel analysis is required. Do not infer organization-wide conditions solely from individual self-reports.

**Research versus practice.** A heuristic may help structure inquiry without constituting a validated causal model. Likewise, research supporting opportunity to perform does not establish HEEn’s distinctive decision value. Retain, merge, narrow, or retire labels when evidence does not support their intended role.

**Reference:** Blumberg, M., & Pringle, C. D. (1982). The missing opportunity in organizational research: Some implications for a theory of work performance. *Academy of Management Review, 7*(4), 560–569. [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.1982.4285240). See the [HEE Core](https://github.com/titumozammel/human-energy-economics) for its broader evidence map and references. The reference establishes an antecedent, not validation of HEEn.

## Research agenda and tests

The following are proposed HEEn study questions, **not newly registered Core propositions**. References to P10, P11, P5, P13, and P15 indicate links to existing core tests, not a claim that these studies have occurred or been preregistered.

| Test | Core proposition | Design and measures | Evidence supporting a bounded claim | Evidence weakening or narrowing it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Diagnostic reliability; linked to P11 | P11 (diagnostic reliability) | Independent assessors code capability, capacity, connection, and enablement separately from a common evidence packet; allow mixed/Unknown; report raw and chance-corrected agreement with intervals | Agreement exceeds a pre-specified meaningful threshold, with categories distinguishable from nearest alternatives | Adequately measured cases cannot be coded reliably; merge or revise distinctions |
| Decision divergence; linked to P10 Stage 0 | P10 Stage 0 (decision divergence) | Compare HEEn-guided choices with a credible existing performance diagnostic using equal information, attention, and expertise | Consistent, justified changes in choices or evidence requests | No meaningful decision difference; narrow novelty to a presentation or navigation aid |
| Intervention usefulness; linked to P10 | P10 (matched-intervention outcomes) | Within supported blocked-application cases, compare a bounded enablement change with a credible alternative or phased implementation; measure application, quality, total cost, harms, and coverage | Improvement exceeds a pre-specified meaningful difference without unacceptable trade-offs | No meaningful advantage in an adequately powered, implemented test; revise incremental usefulness claims |
| Six-pillar organization | None: HEEn-specific | Test coverage and independent coding first; test factor structure only if claiming measurable separate dimensions | Reliable, useful indexing; separable dimensions only if measurement supports them | Persistent ambiguity, redundancy, or omitted barriers; consolidate or replace pillars |
| Pillar reliability [P] | None: HEEn-specific, unregistered | Independent assessors, blind to each other, classify the same evidence packets of blocked-application cases into the six pillars, with “overlap” and “Unknown” allowed; report raw and chance-corrected agreement and a confusion matrix, using a pre-specified decision rule | Agreement above a pre-specified threshold, with few confusions between neighbouring pillars | Persistent confusion or heavy overlap; merge or redefine pillars, and treat the six as an indexing aid only |
| Mechanism | None: HEEn-specific (relates to P10) | Independently measure the restriction and implementation before measuring application; use comparison and temporal data | The restriction changes before application improves, with alternatives addressed | Application improves without the proposed change, or the restriction changes with no meaningful application effect; narrow the mechanism claim |
| Protection and sustainability; linked to P5/P13 | P5 and P13 (protection, reabsorption) | Define release, ownership, destination, reassignment rules, and horizon; compare relevant protection arrangements and repeated coverage | Explicit protection changes reabsorption or coverage under specified demand conditions | No meaningful difference under an adequate test; do not treat any redeployment as failure |
| Reader utility; linked to P15 | P15 (decision aids) | Representative role-based tasks using this document versus a credible comparator; predefine completion-time and blinded quality criteria | Faster answers without a pre-specified unacceptable quality loss, or better quality at acceptable effort | Speed worsens, important answers cannot be found, or decision quality falls; revise routing and aids |

### Minimum study protocol

1. **Claim, unit, and setting:** specify the hypothesis, task/workflow/person/organization level, population, horizon, and applicability boundaries.
2. **Operational definitions:** measure capability independently of outcomes; distinguish available capacity from access; define the restriction without using poor performance as its only evidence.
3. **Comparison:** choose a credible existing approach or justified control; balance information, attention, and expertise. Do not manufacture a weak comparator by knowingly assigning useless training to a clearly blocked group.
4. **Outcomes:** predefine primary application and quality outcomes, costs, harms, implementation checks, and future-coverage measures. Do not infer energy directly from waiting or ticket counts.
5. **Design:** use randomization where feasible and justified; otherwise state the observational or quasi-experimental design, confounding controls, and attribution limits. Plan for clustering and repeated measures.
6. **Decision rules:** pre-specify minimum meaningful effects, uncertainty intervals, sample-size rationale, missing-data handling, exclusions, and criteria for support, contradiction, or inconclusive evidence.
7. **Governance:** obtain necessary permissions and consent, minimize identifying data, disclose conflicts, and use independent assessment and analysis where feasible. Separate intervention delivery from outcome rating.
8. **Registration and reporting:** preregister only by depositing a real protocol with an accessible registry record before the relevant data are examined. Report null, adverse, mixed, and contrary results, deviations, and limitations.

An imprecise result or failed implementation is generally inconclusive about efficacy, not confirmation. A sufficiently precise null or contrary result after a fair test should narrow or reject the specified claim. Do not rescue a failed claim by changing definitions after seeing outcomes; revised claims require new testing. “Enablement always helps” is not a substitute for specifying where, how much, and compared with what.

**Boundary studies.** External shocks, absent capability, severe capacity shortage, legitimate safeguards, or downstream bottlenecks may limit benefit. A lack of observed boundary differences can challenge a particular moderation claim; it does not by itself refute all enablement effects. Longitudinal follow-up needs repeated measures and a stated durability threshold, not merely the passage of time.
